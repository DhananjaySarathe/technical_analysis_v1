Section 01

Foundations — read one candle before you read a thousand

Everything in this book is built from four numbers: Open, High, Low, Close. A candle is a compressed battle report of buyers versus sellers for one unit of time. Learn to read the report and every pattern later becomes obvious instead of memorized.

Section 02

Single-Candle Signals

One candle can flag a shift — but only where it appears. A hammer in the middle of nowhere is noise; a hammer at support after a stretched decline is information. Every card states the required context. Reliability dots (● ● ●) are a rough guide assuming correct context + confirmation.

Section 03

Two-Candle Signals

Two-candle patterns show a hand-over of control — one side finishes its move, the other side answers. The size of the answer, and the volume behind it, tells you how serious the new side is.

Section 04

Three-Candle & Multi-Candle Signals

Three-candle patterns tell a complete story: climax → pause → reversal. They are slower to appear but generally more reliable than single candles, because the market has had time to prove the turn.

Section 05

Chart Patterns

Chart patterns are crowd psychology drawn over days and weeks. Each one has three tradeable parts: the boundary line (neckline / trendline), the measured target (pattern height projected from the break), and a volume signature that separates real breaks from traps. The volume pane below each chart is part of the pattern — never read one without it.

Section 06

Volume & Hidden Tells

This is the part most beginners skip and most professionals live on. Price tells you what happened; volume tells you who was behind it and how committed they were. These are the quiet observations — effort vs result, dry-ups, springs, climaxes — that turn patterns from decoration into edge.

Section 07

The Playbook — turning patterns into trades

Pattern recognition is maybe 20% of trading. The other 80% is context, entry discipline, position sizing and exits. This is the part that decides whether the same knowledge makes or loses money.

Educational reference only. Nothing here is investment advice or a recommendation to buy or sell any security. Patterns describe probabilities, not certainties — the same setup wins and loses depending on market regime, timeframe and execution. Backtest, paper-trade, size small, and always know your exit before your entry.
